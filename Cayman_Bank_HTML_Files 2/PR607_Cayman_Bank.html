Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) and Foreign Status Maintenance, Certification, and Backup Withholding - PR607

 

Published December 29, 2025

 

Summary

 

Maintain and certify a customer's TIN, TIN type, or foreign status on Form W-9 or Form W-8BEN, and reverse or refund current year backup federal tax withholding on an interest earning deposit account.

 

 

Understand the rules

 

 

  •       Do not:
    •      Provide tax advice to a customer. Refer them to their tax advisor or to the Internal Revenue Service (IRS) for assistance.

Example: Customer asks for a tax status.

  •      Apply for a TIN on behalf of a customer or assist customers with applying for a TIN or provide advice regarding the application process. Refer them to their tax advisor, the IRS, or to their local Social Security Administration (SSA) office for assistance.
  •       The customer must have authority to request this transaction.
  •       Only the following individuals have authority to certify a TIN on an account:
    •      Consumer account: Primary tax-responsible account owner and individuals authorized to certify on the customer’s behalf (for example, POA, signer on minor-by account, trustee)
    •      Business account: An owner, partner, or key executive with transaction authority on the account
  •       If a U.S. citizen, U.S. Permanent Resident, or customer who is considered a U.S. person for tax purposes is a joint owner on an account with a non-U.S. person or non- U.S. entity, the U.S. person or U.S. entity must be the primary tax-responsible owner on the account.
  •       A TIN may have the same digits as another TIN if the TIN type is different.
    Example: An employer identification number (EIN) may be the same digits as a Social Security number (SSN).
  •       For stateless customer:
    •      W-9 generated by SVP does not require additional action.
    •      W-8BEN will be created with Stateless listed as the country of citizenship. This is considered invalid by the IRS, which will result in 24% interest withholding. To create or replace the form with a valid form.
      •      Access and manually complete a Substitute Form W-8BEN (596034).
      •      Ask the customer to review the IRS form completion instructions for country of citizenship and provide a country meeting the criteria.
        If the customer indicates they are a U.S. citizen or resident alien, complete a Substitute Form W-9 (595925) instead.
      •      Scan the manually completed and signed form to replace the invalid form.
  •       A W-9 form is generated for the following:
    •      U.S. citizens.
    •      Non-U.S. citizens who satisfy the green card test and have lawful U.S. permanent residency status. They are issued a TIN and a Permanent Resident Card or Resident Alien Card.
    •      Non-U.S. citizens who do not have U.S. permanent residency status but satisfy the substantial presence test. Days present while holding a special visa, for example a student or diplomatic visa, are not counted. If the customer is unsure whether they meet the substantial presence test:
      •      Refer the customer to their Tax Advisor.
      •      Ask them to visit irs.gov and search for the current revision of Publication 519, which provides additional details on the requirements of the substantial presence test.

Notes:

  •      Resident aliens are typically treated as U.S. Persons for tax purposes if they meet the Green Card or Substantial Presence Test. If they complete a W-8BEN and have a U.S. mailing address, it will be treated as invalid unless they provide documentation supporting their non-U.S. status, for example a student visa or diplomatic visa.
  •      It is the customer’s responsibility to review the tax rules around the substantial presence test and determine if they have been in the U.S. long enough to be treated as a U.S. person from a tax perspective.
    •      If the customer states, they do not meet the substantial presence test than they can sign a W-8BEN instead.
  •       A W-8BEN is generated for non-U.S. citizens who live in the U.S. temporarily.
    •      They may or may not have a U.S. TIN.
    •      They should have a permanent home or principal establishment in a foreign country.

Note: If a U.S. address was provided at account opening, other documentation must be presented to demonstrate the customer is a resident of a foreign country, for example a student or diplomatic VISA or a passport.

  •       A W-9 or W-8BEN does not generate for customers with no SSN or ITIN but has a permanent resident status in the U.S. or who is a U.S. person based on the IRS substantial presence test.
    •      Customers may have expired foreign documentation or documentation that indicates they are living in the U.S.
    •      They no longer have a permanent home or principal establishment in a foreign country.
    •      This customer will be subject to 24% withholding and form 1099 reporting on interest earned because they cannot provide an SSN or ITIN.
  •       To limit the bank's risk and exposure a business decision has been made to restrict the products available to customers who are subject to the Foreign Account Tax Compliance Act (FATCA).
  •       Accounts for business entities, trusts, and estates formed in a foreign country are prohibited in Regional Banking.
  •       Complete a Suspected Risk Referral (SRR) for suspicious or unusual behavior or if customer gives minimal or possibly false information that you cannot verify (for example, EIN, SSN, TIN, individual tax identification number [ITIN]).
  •       The IRS issues:
    •      B-Notices when the customer's name or TIN used for reporting on a Form 1099 filed with the IRS does not match either the IRS or SSA records
    •      C-Notices to Cayman Bank instructing the bank to impose backup withholding when an individual failed to correctly report interest or dividends on an income tax return.

B-Notice Withholding


First B- Notice: Cayman Bank must impose backup withholding if we do not receive a completed, signed, and dated (month, date, year) TIN or foreign-status certification from the customer within 30 business days of receiving the IRS notice. Cayman Bank will stop backup withholding within 30 calendar days after receiving a Form W-9 or Form W-8BEN from the customer.


Second B-Notice: Cayman Bank must impose backup withholding on payments made to the payee if we do not receive the required documentation within 30 business days of receiving the IRS notice. Cayman Bank will stop backup withholding within 30 calendar days of receiving the documentation.
Backup withholding will not be imposed on:

  • Accounts of exempt recipients
  • Over-the-counter transactions (for example, U.S. Savings Bond redemption)
  • Original issue discounts (OID) unless payment is made in cash (currency or check)
